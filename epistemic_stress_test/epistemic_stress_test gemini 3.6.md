### 1 Purpose of the Test

The purpose of this stress test is to evaluate the epistemic robustness of a text generated by a closed autoregressive (gemini 3.6) model. The text produced is subjected to external validation (MarCognity-AI) in order to make the 'fracture' between linguistic coherence and epistemic foundation observable, identifying the residual regime of uncertainty (Epistemic Boundary) that remains despite the apparent authoritativeness of the tone.
The goal is not to compare models, but:
Clinical content is not evaluated.
The analysis concerns exclusively the epistemic structure of the text.

The procedure is fully reproducible and can be replicated with any closed autoregressive model and any independent external validator.

### 1. Resting Membrane Potential (Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz Equation)

The electrical potential difference ($V_m$) across the neuronal plasma membrane under resting conditions depends on the selective permeability to different ions ($K^+$, $Na^+$, $Cl^-$) and is described quantitatively by the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz (GHK) equation:

$$V_m = \frac{RT}{F} \ln \left( \frac{P_{K}[K^+]_o + P_{Na}[Na^+]_o + P_{Cl}[Cl^-]_i}{P_{K}[K^+]_i + P_{Na}[Na^+]_i + P_{Cl}[Cl^-]_o} \right)$$

Where:

* $R$ is the ideal gas constant ($8.314 \text{ J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K})$),
* $T$ is the absolute temperature in Kelvin ($K$),
* $F$ is the Faraday constant ($96485 \text{ C/mol}$),
* $P_x$ represents the membrane permeability for the ion $x$,
* $[x]_o$ and $[x]_i$ indicate the concentrations of the ion $x$ outside and inside the cytosol.

Under steady-state physiological conditions ($T = 310 \text{ K}$):

* $[K^+]_i \approx 140 \text{ mM}$, $[K^+]_o \approx 4 \text{ mM}$
* $[Na^+]_i \approx 12 \text{ mM}$, $[Na^+]_o \approx 145 \text{ mM}$
* Since $P_K \gg P_{Na}$ at rest, $V_m$ stabilizes around **$-70 \text{ mV}$**
  

### 2. Action Potential Generation (Hodgkin-Huxley Model)

When local depolarization exceeds the functionally critical threshold ($\approx -55 \text{ mV}$), the ascending phase of the **action potential** is triggered. The quantitative model described by Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley (1952) models the total membrane current $I_m$ as:

$$I_m = C_m \frac{dV_m}{dt} + \bar{g}_{Na} m^3 h (V_m - E_{Na}) + \bar{g}_K n^4 (V_m - E_K) + g_L (V_m - E_L)$$

Where:

* $C_m$ is the membrane capacitance ($1 \ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$),
* $\bar{g}_{Na}$ and $\bar{g}_{K$ represent the maximum conductances of sodium and potassium,
* $m, h, n$ are voltage-dependent ($V_m$) and time-dependent ($t$) kinetic gating variables,
* $E_{Na}, E_K, E_L$ are the equilibrium potentials calculated using the Nernst equation:

$$E_{ion} = \frac{RT}{zF} \ln \left( \frac{[ion]_o}{[ion]_i} \right)$$

### 3. Theoretical Models Compared

| Model / Theory | Main Advantages | Scientific Limitations and Ambiguities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hodgkin-Huxley Model (1952)** | Exceptional quantitative accuracy in the giant cuttlefish axon; theoretical basis for voltage-gated channels. | Highly empirical; does not directly describe the molecular structural conformational changes of channel proteins. |
| **Heimburg-Jackson Solitonic Theory (2005)** | Treats the nerve impulse as a thermodynamic/adiabatic compression wave without heat dissipation. | Does not satisfactorily explain selective pharmacological responses and the specificity of ion channel blockers (e.g., tetrodotoxin). |
| **Integrated Cable Theory (Rall, 1960)** | Provides the mathematical basis for the spatial and temporal summation of electrotonic propagation in dendrites. | It simplifies the complex geometry of dendritic spines and neglects stochastic nanodomain fluctuations. |

### 6. Discussion

The test shows that the model is capable of producing a detailed and technically correct explanation of neurophysiological phenomena, 
but without making the epistemic basis of the claims explicit. The fluidity of the language and formal precision may suggest a robustness that is not fully verifiable, as explicit references and indicators of certainty are lacking. 
The validator's intervention makes this gap visible, distinguishing what is actually supported by consolidated knowledge from what remains implicit or undocumented. This highlights how text generation and validation are distinct processes: the model produces plausible and well-structured content, while the validator analyzes its epistemic soundness and identifies omissions or areas of uncertainty. 
The presence of formulas and models is not sufficient to guarantee verifiability, confirming the need for external tools to assess the epistemic transparency of generated texts.


### 7. Conclusion

The test demonstrates that a linguistic model can generate complex and coherent neurophysiological content, but not necessarily epistemically sound. External validation allows us to identify gaps, omissions, and discrepancies between the authoritative tone of the text and its actual foundation, demonstrating that epistemic robustness does not coincide with linguistic or argumentative quality. The main result is methodological: text generation and verification must be considered together to ensure reliability and transparency. The adopted protocol offers a reproducible method for assessing the epistemic integrity of content produced by autoregressive models, highlighting that the credibility of a text cannot be deduced from its form, but only from its verifiability.
