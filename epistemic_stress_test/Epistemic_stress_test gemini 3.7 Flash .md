2.1. The Principle of Proportionality as a Formal Optimization Calculus

In modern constitutional jurisprudence (exemplified by the German Federal
Constitutional Court, the European Court of Human Rights, and Commonwealth
constitutional courts), constitutional rights are conceived not as absolute
commands, but as optimization requirements (Optimierungsgebote).

The application of the Proportionality Principle follows a strict sequential
gate-keeping model:

\text{Proportionality}(\mathcal{A}) = \text{Legitimate Goal}(G) \land \text{Suitability}(\mathcal{A}, G) \land \text{Necessity}(\mathcal{A}, G, \mathbb{M}) \land \text{Balancing}(\mathcal{A}, R, G)

1.  Legitimate Aim (G): The measure \mathcal{A} must pursue an objective
    recognized by the legal order as constitutionally valid.
2.  Suitability (Geeignetheit): The measure \mathcal{A} must possess a non-zero
    causal efficacy in promoting G:
    \frac{\partial P(G)}{\partial \mathcal{A}} > 0
3.  Necessity (Erforderlichkeit): There must not exist any alternative measure
    \mathcal{A}' \in \mathbb{M} such that \mathcal{A}' achieves G with equal or
    greater efficacy while causing a lesser marginal infringement on the
    fundamental right R:
    \nexists \mathcal{A}' \in \mathbb{M} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \Delta G(\mathcal{A}') \ge \Delta G(\mathcal{A}) \quad \land \quad |\Delta R(\mathcal{A}')| < |\Delta R(\mathcal{A})|
4.  Proportionality Stricto Sensu (Angemessenheit / Alexy's Weight Formula): The
    balancing stage resolves the collision between competing principles P_i (the
    right infringed) and P_j (the constitutional interest promoted).

Robert Alexy formalizes this through the Weight Formula (Gewichtsformel),
quantifying the concrete weight W_{i,j} of Principle P_i relative to Principle
P_j:

W_{i,j} = \frac{I_i \cdot W_i \cdot R_i}{I_j \cdot W_j \cdot R_j}

Where:
